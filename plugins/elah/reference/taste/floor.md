# The floor: what makes a frame look cheap

Always on, under every taste. Adapted from the MIT-licensed taste skills (see `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`), rewritten for video.

- **No emoji as artwork.** A drawn shape or a vocabulary icon, or nothing.
- **One accent, on about a tenth of the frame.** No rainbow, no multi-hue gradients, no second glow colour.
- **Two faces at most:** one display or serif, one sans. Never a third.
- **Do not centre everything.** Hang the layout from a margin or a grid edge; let one edge carry the weight. A lone logo may centre.
- **No hard dark drop shadows.** Depth comes from scale, overlap, contrast and a soft low-opacity blur, never a black offset copy.
- **No stock-cliche icons** (lightbulb, rocket, gears, handshake, trophy) unless the brief names them. Prefer an icon that says the
  specific thing, or a drawn shape.
- **One hero.** One thing the eye goes to first; everything else is smaller, quieter and gets out of its way.
- **No generated-film tells:** a screen of three identical cards in a row; the same icon-in-a-circle on every line; titles that keep
  moving after they landed; one entrance repeated on every element; a side-by-side pair standing in for a scene; a logo parked in a corner;
  opening on a black frame or the logo; copy that starts "Introducing" or "Meet".
- **No filler copy.** Short, specific, real: the user's names and numbers, never an invented statistic, customer, award or quote.
  Cut words before you shrink type.
- **Contrast first.** Body text must read against whatever sits behind it at that moment; accent text is large text.
